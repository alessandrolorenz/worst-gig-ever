# M13.1 — Groove Readability & Entry Tuning

## Status

Owner-authorized tuning milestone based on the first physical M13 rhythm playtest.

## Objective

Improve the player's ability to visually monitor and physically hit the Groove Pad while simultaneously defending against incoming objects.

This milestone must preserve the successful difficulty created by the Groove + Defense combination.

The problem being solved is **ergonomic/readability difficulty**, not gameplay difficulty.

## Owner playtest findings

The owner reported:

- the new dual-task loop is fun;
- it is difficult to maintain Groove and break bottles at the same time;
- that difficulty is desirable and feels like part of the game's identity;
- the player currently has to move visual attention too far between Groove and Defense;
- a larger lower-center Groove Pad should help the player keep both tasks inside one visual focus area;
- a countdown would improve preparation before the chaos starts;
- a future two-pad mode could increase difficulty later, but is not part of this milestone.

## Product principle

Preserve:

> **Keep the beat. Survive the gig.**

The challenge should come from coordinating two tasks, not from fighting the interface.

M13.1 removes unnecessary visual/entry friction while keeping the core challenge intact.

---

# 1. Groove Pad Placement

## Required direction

Move the Groove Pad to the **lower center** of the gameplay scene, visually integrated with the drum-kit foreground.

The player's default gaze should be able to monitor:

- the Groove pulse;
- the central projectile corridor;
- approaching bottles/mugs;

without large horizontal eye movements.

## Reference-canvas guidance

The project uses a 1920 × 1080 reference canvas.

The pad center should be close to the horizontal center of that canvas.

Starting guidance:

- target X: approximately the visual center (`~960`);
- lower gameplay region;
- visually connected to the drum kit;
- not attached to the HUD;
- not rendered as a floating generic UI button.

Claude must inspect the current drum-kit composition and choose the exact geometry based on the actual scene.

The implementation return must record:

- previous pad center / size / hit area;
- new pad center / size / hit area;
- why the final position was selected.

## Z-order

The Groove Pad should feel part of the drum kit.

However:

- incoming targets must remain readable when crossing the pad region;
- target visuals should render above any broad pulse glow when necessary;
- the pulse must never hide bottles/mugs;
- Groove feedback text must not cover the central target corridor.

---

# 2. Groove Pad Size & Hit Area

## Intent

Make the pad meaningfully easier to visually acquire and tap while multitasking.

This is not a timing-window change.

## Guidance

A roughly 30–50% increase from the current visual/tappable footprint is a reasonable starting point, **not a mandatory numeric contract**.

Claude should derive the final size from:

- current phone-scale readability;
- drum-kit geometry;
- reference-canvas safe space;
- target-path readability;
- touch ergonomics.

## Requirements

- visual pad and tappable area must stay aligned;
- the hit area must be config-driven;
- no nested `Pressable`;
- preserve the existing M5A/M10/M11 surface-coordinate input pipeline;
- no pointer-event regression;
- visual art bounds must not become gameplay truth.

## Visual identity

The current production drum-kit image may remain unchanged.

If the current art does not contain a suitable lower-center cymbal, M13.1 may render a lightweight code-driven cymbal/pad treatment layered into the drum-kit foreground.

No new production art pack is required for this milestone.

M14 may later replace the drum kit with art that naturally incorporates the Groove cymbal.

---

# 3. Pulse Readability

The pulse remains code-driven.

It must communicate the expected tap moment using at least two perceptual cues, such as:

- scale;
- brightness/glow;
- ring expansion;
- brief hit flash.

Do not rely on color alone.

## Important

Increasing the pad size must not change:

- BPM;
- beat schedule;
- PERFECT window;
- GOOD window;
- Groove score values.

The pulse is easier to see, not easier to time.

---

# 4. Pre-Round Countdown

## Objective

Give the player a short, deterministic preparation period before Defense begins.

## Sequence

Use four beat-aligned countdown steps:

```text
3
2
1
GO
```

Each step occurs on consecutive beats of the **existing visual beat clock**.

The Groove Pad visibly pulses on every countdown beat.

## Countdown timing

Countdown uses the current Groove BPM.

Do not create a second independent timer.

Do not use:

- `setInterval`;
- `setTimeout` as gameplay truth;
- wall-clock time;
- audio playback position.

Countdown must derive from the same deterministic elapsed-time model used by Groove.

## Relationship to the existing M10 count-in

M13.1 **replaces the old two-beat scored-preparation/count-in behavior** with the explicit `3 → 2 → 1 → GO` pre-roll.

Do not stack the old two-beat count-in after the new countdown.

There must be one coherent preparation sequence.

## Gameplay boundary

Before the `GO` boundary:

- no bottle/mug spawning;
- no target misses;
- no Show Integrity changes;
- no Defense score/combo activity;
- no Groove score;
- no Groove misses;
- no Groove streak changes;
- no vocalist event progression.

At the `GO` boundary:

- normal round gameplay begins;
- round elapsed gameplay time starts/continues from its defined zero point;
- Defense spawning becomes eligible;
- vocalist timeline becomes eligible.

The `GO` pulse itself is **not scored**.

The first scored Groove beat occurs exactly one beat interval after `GO`.

This gives the player a clean transition from preparation to active multitasking.

---

# 5. Round State Semantics

## Preferred model

Represent countdown explicitly in round/domain state if that fits the current architecture cleanly.

A preferred state machine is:

```text
READY
  ↓
COUNTDOWN
  ↓
PLAYING
  ⇄
PAUSED
```

with existing vocalist and terminal transitions preserved.

If the current architecture has a cleaner equivalent pre-roll phase, Claude may use it, but the behavioral contract above is mandatory.

## Countdown start

The player's existing Start action begins the countdown.

Do not require a second confirmation after countdown.

## Restart

Restart begins from the same pre-round sequence:

```text
READY → COUNTDOWN → PLAYING
```

or the current product's equivalent title/restart flow.

No stale rhythm/countdown state may survive a restart.

---

# 6. Background / Pause Behavior

## During COUNTDOWN

If the app backgrounds during countdown:

- cancel the in-progress countdown;
- return to the safe pre-round READY state;
- no score, miss, integrity, or target consequence occurs.

Reason:

Resuming halfway through a 3-second preparation sequence would be confusing and provides no gameplay value.

## During PLAYING

Preserve the existing auto-pause behavior exactly.

## Explicit pause

Do not add a new pause control to COUNTDOWN unless one already exists naturally.

---

# 7. Difficulty Freeze

M13.1 must not change:

- `90 BPM`;
- PERFECT ±90 ms;
- GOOD ±180 ms;
- bottle/mug spawn cadence;
- target phase speeds;
- fastball behavior;
- hitbox forgiveness;
- duplicate-touch suppression;
- Show Integrity;
- vocalist timing;
- round duration;
- Defense scoring/combo;
- Groove scoring values.

If implementation appears too hard after the ergonomic changes, that is a new playtest finding, not permission to silently tune these values.

---

# 8. Future Difficulty — Second Groove Area

Document only.

Do not implement.

Potential future progression:

```text
Stage 1: one central Groove Pad + Defense
Stage 2: faster/more Defense pressure
Stage 3: two Groove zones
Stage 4: alternating Groove zones + Defense
```

The current architecture should not be unnecessarily hard-coded to make a second pad impossible later, but M13.1 remains a **single-pad** game.

---

# 9. HUD / Copy

Countdown text should be visually central and concise.

Preferred:

```text
3
2
1
GO!
```

It may use the current game typography.

Requirements:

- does not permanently shift HUD layout;
- disappears cleanly at gameplay start;
- does not obscure the Groove Pad pulse;
- countdown and pulse visually feel related.

No additional tutorial modal is required.

---

# 10. Audio

No new audio synchronization.

The existing music remains independent of the visual beat clock.

Optional:

- existing one-shot feedback may remain on successful Groove taps;
- countdown does not require new audio assets.

Do not add new dependencies or generate audio for M13.1.

---

# 11. Testing Requirements

M13.1 must add deterministic tests for:

- countdown phase progression;
- exact `3 → 2 → 1 → GO` ordering;
- countdown derived from BPM;
- no scoring during countdown;
- no Groove misses during countdown;
- no target spawning during countdown;
- no Show Integrity change during countdown;
- vocalist timeline does not progress during countdown;
- GO transition happens exactly once;
- first scored beat occurs one interval after GO;
- restart resets countdown/rhythm state;
- background during countdown returns to READY;
- normal PLAYING background/pause behavior is unchanged;
- pad center/size/hit geometry remains inside safe reference-canvas bounds;
- tap inside new pad resolves Groove;
- tap outside pad does not;
- overlapping target/pad input behavior from M11 remains valid;
- multi-touch behavior remains valid;
- deterministic behavior remains frame-rate independent.

# 12. Native Validation

When Android tooling is available, physically validate:

1. countdown starts after Start;
2. pad pulses during all four countdown steps;
3. no bottle appears before GO;
4. central pad is visibly easier to monitor;
5. pad is comfortably tappable;

# Exit Criteria

M13.1 is complete when:

- the Groove Pad is clearly larger and lower-center;
- the pad remains visually part of the drum-kit experience;
- countdown is deterministic and beat-aligned;
- there is one preparation sequence, not countdown + old count-in;
- no gameplay difficulty values changed;
- dual-task input contracts still pass;
- all automated gates pass;
- native smoke passes when available;
- the build is ready for owner re-test.

Final milestone state:

`READY_FOR_M13_1_RETEST`
